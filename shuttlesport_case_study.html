Overview

A beloved club. A booking process
that gets in the way.

Shuttlesport Badminton Academy is a local badminton and pickleball club in Port Coquitlam, BC. Despite strong community engagement, their existing website made it difficult for players and new members to discover, register for, and pay for classes — creating unnecessary friction at every step of the experience.

The Brief
Redesign the Shuttlesport website to reduce friction in the class booking journey — from initial discovery through schedule browsing, registration, waiver completion, and payment.
The Solution
A streamlined booking flow with a redesigned activity calendar, an integrated cart and booking summary, an in-line waiver form, and a consolidated payment screen — all tied to a personal user profile.

Research

Understanding the club — from the inside out.

We began with ethnographic research at Shuttlesport itself — attending drop-in sessions, observing how members navigated the existing website, and speaking informally with players and coaches. This grounded our understanding in what was actually happening at the club, not just what users said in interviews.

From this research we identified two distinct user groups who would shape our entire design direction: Kim, a recreational badminton and pickleball player representing the club's primary member base, and Thomas, a club coach whose perspective would later validate and reinforce our final design decisions.

Design Focus
"How might we redesign the Shuttlesport website experience so that recreational players like Kim can easily discover, register for, and pay for classes — without confusion or unnecessary friction?"
Journey Mapping

Two perspectives.
One design focus.

We mapped the end-to-end experience for both user types. Kim's journey — from first visit to post-registration — revealed clear pain points around schedule clarity, waiver friction, and payment confusion. Thomas's journey as a coach highlighted operational frustrations that, while distinct, ultimately reinforced the same core gaps in the booking flow.

Initial Concepts

Exploring design directions
before narrowing focus.

We developed three initial design concepts — each exploring a different approach to the core booking problem. Concept 1 focused on redesigning the schedule and registration flow. Concept 2 explored a more profile-centric approach to class management. A third concept (social media revamp) was explored and ruled out early as outside the scope of what we could meaningfully redesign.

Reframed Focus

Refining the problem
before designing the solution.

The ethnographic research, journey maps, initial concepts, and client feedback all pointed us toward a more precise design focus. Rather than trying to redesign the entire website, we reframed around the core booking journey: the path a user like Kim takes from discovering an activity to completing registration and payment.

This sharper focus became the lens for everything that followed — the user workshop, the refined concepts, and ultimately the final design.

01
Simplify activity discovery — make it easy to find what's on and when.
02
Reduce registration friction — eliminate unnecessary steps in the booking flow.

User Workshop

Testing assumptions with

With a reframed design focus in place, we ran a structured user workshop to validate our direction and generate insights directly from the people who would use the product. The workshop combined roleplaying scenarios and a Rose, Bud, Thorn exercise to surface honest reactions to the existing experience and our proposed improvements.

Final Design

The complete booking experience —
from first visit to confirmation.

The final design reimagines the Shuttlesport booking journey as a seamless, end-to-end flow. A redesigned activity calendar makes schedule discovery intuitive. A unified cart and booking summary brings together activities and lessons. An integrated waiver and payment screen eliminates the need to leave the booking context. And a personal profile gives members visibility into their upcoming classes at a glance.

01
Step
Initial Touchpoints
Kim visits the Shuttlesport website from home to register for pickleball classes. The redesigned homepage gives her clear entry points to lessons, activities, and club information.
Design Decision
Clear navigation and prominent CTAs on the homepage reduce the time-to-discovery for new and returning users.

Step
Calendar & Schedule
Kim navigates to the Activities page to check when drop-ins, events, and pickleball classes are offered. She taps a Saturday slot to view available time slots, selects her activities, and also browses the Lessons page to register for Shuttle 1.
Design Decision
The calendar view + modal pattern lets users see availability and register without navigating away from their chosen date.

03
Step
Waiver
As Kim moves toward payment, she notices a waiver below. Since it's her first time at Shuttlesport, she fills out the form and submits it — without leaving the booking flow.
Design Decision
Integrating the waiver into the booking flow eliminates a major drop-off point that existed when it was a separate, easy-to-miss step.

04
Step
Payment
Kim reaches the consolidated payment page — a single screen showing her order summary (activities + lesson) alongside payment options and card details. She reviews, fills in her information, and completes the transaction.
Design Decision
Consolidating the cart and payment onto one screen removes the back-and-forth that frustrated users in the existing flow.
×
05
Step
Personal Schedule
After completing payment, Kim checks her profile and sees all her upcoming classes — Badminton Drop-In tomorrow and Shuttle 1 on Thursday — alongside her coach report and achievements. Registration confirmed.
Design Decision
A personal profile page closes the loop — giving users immediate confirmation and ongoing visibility into their schedule.

Reflection

What this project
taught us.

Working directly with a real client and real users forced us to stay grounded. The ethnographic research, journey mapping, and user workshop all reinforced the same core insight: the friction wasn't in any single screen — it was in the gaps between them. The waiver no one noticed. The calendar that showed times but not availability. The payment page that appeared before users had confirmed what they were paying for.

The final design addresses each of those gaps through integration rather than reinvention — keeping the Shuttlesport brand and structure intact while making every step of the booking journey feel like it belongs in the same flow.
